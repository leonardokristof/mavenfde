**Strongest case against the Week 1 verdict.** All four boxes are ticked, and
the questions keep changing shape: a follow-up about the PM, fifteen addresses
on one street, an AAMS number typed as a ticket or a card. With a fixed pipeline
every new kind of question means changing code by hand, while an agent with the
same read-only tools could combine them on its own and cover cases nobody has
written a path for yet.

**Week 3, the non-text content.** Fiberly has none today: everything it reads is database fields and
technicians' notes. The first to arrive would be screenshots that operators paste from AAMS or FIS screens.

1. **Visual, or text as pixels?** Text as pixels. A system screen holds IDs, a status and a task, not a picture anyone has to interpret.
2. **Does the number live only in the pixels?** Yes, the ID or the status on a pasted screen. Nothing has to find the image, because it comes in with the question. OCR inside the network reads it, and code checks the reading: the format, that the ID exists in the address register or in AAMS, and that the record it finds has the address or task shown on the same screen.
3. **Descriptions, or one parse per image?** Neither is needed for search: one OCR pass per screenshot when it is pasted, on the company's approved endpoint, never a batch over a corpus. If field photos ever join the records, the technicians' notes are the descriptions to index first.
4. **Video or audio?** None. A voice message from the field would come in typed out, and the transcript is enough, because the answer needs only the ID or address said in it.

## Week 5 — what the room asked

I presented Session 9 as a recorded video, so no room asked me anything. These are the rubric's questions, answered in writing. Two are marked **fair**, one **open**, and none is confusion, because without a room nobody could misunderstand me.

**On the problem**

- *Who specifically has this problem?* The people at HT who handle fibre connection cases: helpdesk and sales support, regional PMs and AMs, programme managers and field coordinators. To answer one building they open up to six systems.
- *Have you shown this to them?* The representative of field services, operations and construction who asked for it saw the use case and the goldens in Week 1. The business owner of the tool sent five questions of their own in Week 3 and saw the answers. Nobody has seen the refined demo yet; that is Friday, 13 November.
- *What happens if you do nothing, and who absorbs that cost?* **Open.** The operators absorb it, but I cannot say how much, because nobody has timed one lookup by hand. The pilot answers in 24.6 s at the median, which says what the machine takes, not what a person takes. The business owner gets the question on Friday.
- *Is this the biggest problem they have?* I do not know. It is the one they asked for, and I have not compared it with their other problems.

**On the numbers**

- *What is the denominator?* 16 goldens, 22 retrieval labels run three times, 88 harness cases, 15 of them refusals. All generated or invented.
- *Where did the eval set come from?* The goldens were written in Week 1, before the system, in the format of a real export with invented values. The labels were written in Week 3, after the first pipeline, in the shape of the pilot's questions, and five of them are the business owner's own.
- *What does this number not cover?* Real operator messages, real data, and anything at scale.
- *What is the baseline?* `outcome` 0.88 on 88 cases, saved on 9 November. Against the alternatives, agentic RAG and DCI in Week 3, and the MCP agent this week.
- *Would your judge score a wrong answer highly?* Yes, it passed 11 of 14 generated meaning changes. That is why `fields_match` checks the facts without a model and the judge is only a column.

**On the architecture**

- *What did you consider and reject?* MCP, built and measured; Code Mode, measured in Session 10; UTCP, a skill and a subagent, reasoned. The row above has the numbers.
- *What would make you change this?* The internal gateway being ready and an agent on the central platform calling the server; or the systems' answers growing past about 1.7 KB.
- *What breaks first when volume goes up 10×?* Not the bill: about 110 requests a day instead of 11 cost about $1 a month in the pipeline and $2.60 through MCP at today's per-request numbers. The first limit I hit was the local Trino at its 2 GiB memory cap under a burst of thousands of lookups, so at work the question is what the shared Trino allows a service account.
- *How much data does each call return, and what does that cost you?* **Fair.** Until this week I had measured it only on the corpus records. Now it is measured on the systems at the pilot's size and above it, and the answer moved the switch condition.
- *Which part would you have to rewrite to deploy at work?* Only the connection settings and credentials in `systems.py`; the SQL reads the same columns. The real table structure stays in a private repo.

**On the honest bits**

- *What is the worst failure you have seen it produce?* **Fair.** "Mlinska 22 nema optiku" above a record whose status is HOMES CONNECTED, every time in the Week 4 scenarios. An answer that says the opposite of the record it cites is worse than no answer.

- *What would you not let it do unsupervised?* Anything but reading. It never changes a request in AAMS and never answers a customer directly; an operator reads every answer first.

**What I will change for the two fair questions**

- *The worst failure:* an answer that contradicts the status or the project of the record it cites will not go out; the operator gets the record's own text instead. `fields_match` already finds it in the harness, so the same check moves into the endpoint, and the demo on Friday shows Mlinska 22 before and after.
